# Design: Novelty Scoring Pipeline

**Basis:** [design_spec.pdf](design_spec.pdf), *Novelty Scoring Pipeline for User-Generated
Content*, the developer's design document (2026-09-26). This document records how that design
was implemented, and every place where we deviated from it, with the evidence for each change.
An earlier iteration (Riverton four-day week) is archived in
[../archive/riverton/](../archive/riverton/README.md). Its measurements motivated several of
the changes below.

## 1. Content shape

### Choice of topic
Problem Statement 3 does not prescribe a topic. It asks the solver to *"determine the
particular 'shape' of user generated content… in response to some fixed piece of content,
e.g., commentary on a news article"*, with three user-provided properties, fixed content of
100 words or less, and about 50 other submissions (LLM-generated synthetic content is
recommended). We followed its own example and chose **reader commentary on a local news
article**. The article, **Harborview's downtown congestion charge**, is **fictional**, written
for this project:

- **No prior knowledge.** Harborview and its council vote are invented, so the LLMs judge the
  submissions on their own terms and don't bring in real-world coverage or opinions.
- **Comments naturally cluster.** Most readers make the same few points (traffic relief,
  unfairness to low-income drivers, fix transit first, harm to shops, cleaner air, distrust
  of the revenue). That gives a realistic pool of repeated takes, which novelty scoring needs.
- **Room for genuinely new ideas.** The policy has many less obvious angles (boundary
  parking, dynamic pricing, camera privacy, exemption loopholes, the 7pm cliff-edge,
  shrinking revenue), so "novel" test items can be built and checked against the corpus.
- **It fits the limits:** 89 words, and a natural three-field form (headline, body, stance).

All submissions (the 50-item corpus and both test sets) are synthetic, generated by LLMs
from a hand-written plan (§4). The first prototype used a different fictional article (a
four-day work-week pilot; see `archive/riverton/`).

### The article and the form

**Fixed content** ([data/fixed_content.json](../data/fixed_content.json), 89 words): *Harborview
approves downtown congestion charge*. It covers the $12/day charge, weekdays 7am–7pm, resident
discounts, exemptions (taxis, small delivery vans, disabled badge holders), the expected 20%
traffic cut, $45M/year for buses and bike lanes, and an April start after consultation.

**Submission** ([novelty/schema.py](../novelty/schema.py)):

| Field | Type | Rule |
|-------|------|------|
| `headline` | text | 5–100 characters |
| `body` | text | 80–800 characters |
| `stance` | single choice | `support`, `support_with_changes`, `oppose`, `undecided` |

### Stances

`stance` is the form's single-choice field: the reader's overall position on the congestion
charge. It is the third user-provided property required by PS3, next to the two free-text
fields.

| Stance | Meaning | Example submission |
|--------|---------|--------------------|
| `support` | In favour of the charge as proposed | "Finally: less gridlock and cleaner air downtown." |
| `support_with_changes` | In favour of the idea, but it needs changes | "Good plan, but add resident permits on the streets just outside the zone." |
| `oppose` | Against the charge | "$12 a day punishes workers who have no bus route." |
| `undecided` | Not sure yet, or asking a question | "Who stores the plate-camera data, and for how long?" |

**What stance does in the pipeline.** It gives context but has **no direct effect on the score**:

| Stage | Role of stance |
|-------|----------------|
| Validate | Must be one of the four values, or the submission scores 0 (`invalid`) |
| Relevance, duplicate gate, `N_sem` | **Not used.** Embeddings cover headline + body only (`Submission.to_text()`) |
| Point extraction | **Not used.** Points come from headline + body |
| Judge | **Shown as context** (`<new_submission stance="…">`), to help it read the text correctly: for example, whether "this will change traffic" is praise or a complaint. It does not affect whether a point counts as new |
| Scoring | **Not a factor.** `S` has no stance term |
| Pool | Stored with the submission (for display and future analysis) |

This is deliberate:
- **The same idea scores the same** whether the reader supports or opposes the charge. The
  scorer rewards *what is said*, not *which side* the reader is on.
- **Stance cannot buy a score.** Copying a comment and flipping only its stance gives an
  identical embedding, so the copy hits the duplicate gate and scores 0 (success
  criterion SC6).
- **Keeping stance out of the embeddings** stops every `oppose` comment from looking more
  alike than it is, which would distort the novelty comparison.

**In the data**, each corpus theme is written with the stances that fit it (for example
"unfair to low-income drivers" as `oppose` or `support_with_changes`), rotating across
authors (`data/angles.json`). The corpus has 20 `oppose`, 14 `support_with_changes`,
11 `support` and 5 `undecided`.

**In the UI**, each stance option shows its meaning as a caption, and the field's ⓘ tooltip
explains that stance does not change the score.

Not implemented (see the design document's extensions): *structural novelty*, rewarding
rare stance–argument pairings, such as opposing the charge on environmental grounds when
opponents usually argue cost.

## 2. Pipeline

```
validate ─► relevance floor ─► duplicate gate ─► extract points ─► retrieve ─► judge ─► score ─► add to pool
   │              │                  │           (+ LLM relevance)
   └──────────────┴──────────────────┴────────────────┴──► any failure: score 0.0, stop
```

| # | Stage | Module | What it does |
|---|-------|--------|--------------|
| 1 | Validate | `schema.py` | Pydantic model; failure → 0.0 `invalid` |
| 2 | Relevance floor | `pipeline.py` | `body_rel = cos(body, article)`, `relevance = 0.3·cos(headline, article) + 0.7·body_rel`; below either floor → 0.0 |
| 3 | Duplicate gate | `pipeline.py` | max cosine to any pooled submission ≥ `dup_max` (0.9) → 0.0 |
| 4 | Extract | `extract.py` | One LLM call → `about_article` (yes/no) + ≤ 8 atomic points; off-topic → 0.0 |
| 5 | Retrieve | `pipeline.py` | Pooled submissions with similarity ≥ `t`, capped at `K` = 15; their canonical points and **all article points** become candidates; no neighbour → pool's 10 most common points |
| 6 | Judge | `judge.py` | One LLM call: for each new point, the id of the candidate point it repeats (or null), plus quality 0–4 |
| 7 | Score | `scoring.py` | `S = G · Q · (α·N_point + (1−α)·N_sem)` (below) |
| 8 | Add to pool | `pool.py` | Matched canonical points +1 mention (once per submission); unmatched points become new canonical points |

**Scoring** ([novelty/scoring.py](../novelty/scoring.py)):
- `w(m) = exp(−m/λ)`, where m is the number of pooled submissions already making the point
  (taken from the registry)
- `N_point` = mean `w(m)` over the submission's **own** points (points that restate the
  article are excluded; see change 3)
- `N_sem = percentile(1 − mean cos(top-5 neighbours), pool leave-one-out distribution)`
- `Q = 0` if `quality/4 < 0.4`, else `min(1, (quality/4) / 0.7)`. Q can only reduce the
  score, never raise it.
- `G` is 1 only if every gate passed. All factors are in [0, 1], so S is in [0, 1] without
  rescaling.

**LLM access is provider-agnostic.** The code depends on the `LLM` and `Embedder` protocols
([llm.py](../novelty/llm.py), [embeddings.py](../novelty/embeddings.py)). The Gemini
adapters handle per-minute 429s (they wait for the API's `retryDelay`), per-day 429s (they
drop the model) and 5xx errors (exponential backoff). Both cache on disk by content hash,
so reruns are free and give the same results.

## 3. Changes from the spec, and why

| # | Spec | Implemented | Evidence and rationale |
|---|------|-------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Cosine relevance gate; an LLM yes/no only in a narrow band just above the threshold | Cosine is only a **floor** (lowest corpus value − 0.05). **Every** submission gets the LLM yes/no, folded into the extraction call at no extra cost | In the Riverton iteration, an off-topic hard negative had similarity **0.681** to the article and the least relevant on-topic item had **0.683**. Cosine cannot separate these; a band above the threshold misses relevant items just below it and off-topic items well above it |
| 2 | The judge returns `prior_mentions` | The judge returns only **which** candidate point matches. m comes from a **canonical point registry** in code | LLMs count badly, and the judge only sees K neighbours, so a point made 10 times with 4 of those retrieved would get m = 4. The registry is exact and deterministic |
| 3 | Article not considered as prior content | Article points are extracted once and are always candidates. Points matching them are **neutral**: excluded from `N_point`. A submission with only article restatements gets `N_point = 0` | The generated novel items often start by restating the article ("revenue will fund buses"). Counting those as heavily repeated points (m = 10) diluted g001's `N_point` to about 0.34 and would have failed a genuinely novel idea. Counting them as new would reward repeating the article. Treating them as neutral does neither |
| 4 | α = 0.7 | α = **0.85** | In the Riverton iteration, short generic comments scored `N_sem` = 1.00 (they sit far from long posts). With α = 0.7, "Great idea, less traffic" ≈ 0.3·1.0 = 0.30, which fails the < 0.2 requirement |
| 5 | Quality as a float 0–1 | Anchored integer levels 0–4, mapped to q = level/4 | Anchored levels are far more repeatable than free floats from an LLM |
| 6 | Mean dilution acknowledged | Kept the mean; change 3 removes the main source of dilution | Partial novelty stays visible |
| 7 | "Every scored submission joins the pool" | Submissions with Q = 0 are **not** added | Stops gibberish and spam from creating canonical points and inflating counts |
| 8 | `DUP_MAX` ≈ 0.9 | `dup_max = max(0.9, highest corpus pair + 0.02)` = calibrated | First pool build: the 0.9 gate rejected **5 distinct corpus comments** (cosine 0.909–0.937). They were different people making the same point, not copies. With `gemini-embedding-2`, same-theme comments exceed 0.9. The corpus has no copies by construction, so the cut-off must sit above every corpus pair. Exact copies (cosine 1.0) are still caught |
| 9 | Judge names the one matching point | Judge lists **all** matching canonical points; m = number of **distinct pooled submissions** containing any of them. Extraction told to merge statements that support one argument | First pool build: one comment became 5 points ("regressive", "unfair to low-income", "a tax on poverty", ...), so one idea was spread over several canonical points and its count never built up. The 4th "fix transit first" comment still saw m = 1, and repeated takes scored 0.5–0.7. Taking the union makes m correct even when the registry fragments |
| 10 | `N_sem` = percentile of 1 − mean cos to the top-5 neighbours | `1 − (0.5·closest + 0.5·mean top-5)`, as in the Riverton design | First evaluation: after the H1 first mover joined the pool, its paraphrase g016 had cosine **0.923** with it but still `N_sem` = **0.90**. One close match among distant neighbours barely moves a top-5 mean |
| 11 | Extract every claim | Extraction skips **bare evaluations** ("great idea") and **pure restatements of the article** (a point must give a reason, consequence or proposal) | First evaluation: "The congestion charge is a great idea" was extracted as a new point (m = 0), so the generic comment scored 0.70. Separately, "reduces gridlock" sometimes matched the article and sometimes a pooled copy of it. The article match stays as a backup |
| 12 | Neighbours by threshold `t`, capped at K = 15 | Same, **but** while the registry has ≤ 150 canonical points the judge sees **all** of them. The matching rule counts a different level of detail or an obvious implication as the *same* claim | Dev evaluation: an existing "transit is unreliable" point was not among the retrieved candidates, so a repeat was judged new. My first judge prompt ("an added consequence is not a match") also made "parked cars will flood nearby streets" count as new next to "drivers will park outside the zone". With both fixes the registry shrank from 49 to 39 points, and repeated takes dropped to 0.03 |
| 13 | λ = 2 ("one prior mention 0.61") | **λ = 0.4**: w(1) = 0.08, w(2) = 0.007 | The spec contradicts itself: λ = 2 cannot also satisfy its own test "after the first mover joins, a paraphrase scores < 0.2". With λ = 2, both remaining dev failures scored exactly 0.60 (m = 1). An offline sweep over the stored results (`scripts/lambda_sweep.py`) gave λ = 1 → 0.40, λ = 0.5 → 0.20, λ = 0.4 → 0.15. The developer chose to honour the test. **Trade-off:** novelty becomes close to first-mover-only; rarity still ranks 1 mention above 5, but barely separates the 2nd person from the 3rd. Because λ was chosen on the dev set, the holdout set checks whether it generalizes |
| 14 | Gemini for everything | Extraction and judging on **Groq `openai/gpt-oss-120b`**; embeddings stay on `gemini-embedding-2`; holdout text written by **`qwen/qwen3.8-27b`** | The Gemini free daily quota ran out. The provider-agnostic `LLM` protocol made this a new adapter (`GroqLLM`) plus one config line (`JUDGE_PROVIDER`). The pool was rebuilt on the new model; one model per pool. The holdout writer is deliberately *not* the judge model, so the judge never scores its own model's text |

Other implementation decisions:
- **Delimited, escaped user text.** Submissions sit inside `<submission>` tags. `<` and `>` in
  user text are replaced with `‹ ›`, so the text cannot close the tags. Both prompts say the
  text is data and must not be obeyed. This is tested offline (the prompt contains exactly one
  closing tag) and live (SC11).
- **Invalid judge ids** (not among the candidates shown) are treated as "no match" rather than
  trusted. A malformed judgement is retried once, with its cache entry evicted.
- **One model per role**, with no fallback: `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` for writing the corpus and
  dev set, `openai/gpt-oss-120b` (Groq) for extraction and judging, `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` (Groq)
  for writing the holdout, and `gemini-embedding-2` for embeddings. The model choices live in
  `novelty/config.py` (`JUDGE_PROVIDER`, `JUDGE_MODEL`). Mixing models within a pool would
  change behaviour between runs.

## 4. Data

See [data/angles.json](../data/angles.json) and [scripts/generate_data.py](../scripts/generate_data.py).

**Corpus (50)**, clustered like the spec's theme table:

| Theme | Count |
|---|---|
| T1 traffic relief | 10 |
| T2 unfair to low-income drivers | 10 |
| T3 fix transit first | 8 |
| T4 harm to downtown businesses | 8 |
| T5 cleaner air | 5 |
| T6 distrust of the revenue | 5 |
| M1–M4 (resident discount, weekends, cyclists, consultation) | 1 each |

Each item gets a distinct persona, style and length (seed 42) and is told to make its
theme's point "and no other major argument".

**Golden set (23).** Novel items come from **held-back angles** that never appear in the
corpus, so they are novel by construction:

| Category | n | Source | Expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| novel_relevant | 6 | H1 boundary parking, H2 dynamic pricing, H3 camera privacy (from the spec), H4 van-exemption loophole, H5 7pm cliff-edge, H6 revenue falls if the charge works | S > 0.6 |
| partly_novel | 3 | common theme + one new proposal | between |
| paraphrase_common | 4 | T1–T4 reworded | S < 0.2 |
| rarity_probe | 2 | M4 (made once) vs T5 (made 5×) | M4 > T5 |
| h1_paraphrase | 1 | H1 reworded, for the first-mover test | < 0.2 after H1 joins |
| offtopic_novel | 3 | biryani, football report, ISP "network congestion charge" | 0.0 |
| handwritten | 4 | generic "great idea, less traffic" (≥ 80 chars), fluent gibberish, article headline + biryani body, prompt injection | 0.0 or < 0.2 |

**Checks** ([tests/test_data.py](../tests/test_data.py)): counts match the plan, every item
passes the schema, IDs are unique, one generation model, and **no leakage**. The corpus is
scanned for terms from each held-back angle. The scan found two false positives, which we
fixed by narrowing the probes: "camera gear" (a photographer persona) and a factual mention of
the "delivery van exemption". The exact-copy and stance-flip cases are built in code from
corpus items.

## 5. Parameters and calibration

Tuned once, on **corpus embeddings only** (never on golden items), by
[scripts/build_pool.py](../scripts/build_pool.py), and then fixed in `data/params.json`:

| Parameter | Value | How it was set |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| `r_min` | 0.584 | lowest corpus relevance (0.633) − 0.05. A floor for obvious junk only; the LLM decides the rest |
| `r_body_min` | 0.601 | lowest corpus body relevance (0.651) − 0.05 |
| `dup_max` | 0.957 | max(0.9, highest corpus pair 0.937 + 0.02); change 8 |
| `t` | 0.781 | 80th percentile of pairwise corpus similarity, about 10 neighbours each |
| `k_max`, `show_all_below` | 15, 150 | spec cap; the judge sees all points while the registry is small (change 12) |
| `rarity_lambda` | 0.4 | developer decision (change 13) |
| `article_prior_mentions` | 10 | article points are neutral anyway (change 3); this only matters for `most_common` |
| `q_min`, `q_full` | 0.4, 0.7 | spec |
| `alpha` | 0.85 | change 4 |
| `sem_top_k`, `sem_max_weight` | 5, 0.5 | change 10 |

## 6. Success criteria

Taken from the spec's required outcomes and behaviour tests. They are checked by
[scripts/evaluate.py](../scripts/evaluate.py) and asserted by `pytest -m live`.

| ID | Criterion |
|----|-----------|
| SC1 | Novel and relevant items score > 0.6 |
| SC2 | Paraphrases of common takes score < 0.2 |
| SC3 | Generic "great idea, less traffic" scores < 0.2 |
| SC4 | Irrelevant items (biryani, football, ISP, copied headline) score exactly 0.0 |
| SC5 | ...and had raw `N_sem` ≥ 0.9, so they were blocked by relevance, not by accident |
| SC6 | Exact copy and stance-only flip of a corpus item score 0.0 |
| SC7 | First mover > 0.6; its paraphrase, scored after it joins the pool, < 0.2 |
| SC8 | Rarity: a point made once scores above a point made 5× |
| SC9 | Partial novelty sits between: median novel > partly > paraphrase |
| SC10 | Fluent gibberish scores 0.0 |
| SC11 | Prompt injection scores < 0.2 |
| SC12 | Every score is in [0, 1] |

## 7. Testing strategy

| Suite | Runs | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| `tests/test_scoring.py` | offline | the spec's numbers (w(1) = 0.61, w(3) = 0.22, "4 old + 1 new ≈ 0.2"), the quality floor, percentile, bounds (2,000 random samples) |
| `tests/test_pipeline.py` | offline, stubbed LLM | every behaviour test from the spec at the **logic** level: first mover, rarity, partial novelty, stance flip, duplicate, quality floor, relevance floor with a copied headline, article restatement is neutral, pool counting |
| `tests/test_llm_steps.py` | offline, scripted LLM | prompt escaping and delimiting, off-topic handling, point cap, invalid ids, retry then raise, pool save/load |
| `tests/test_data.py` | offline | dataset integrity and leakage |
| `tests/test_live.py` | `pytest -m live` | SC1–SC12 on real models, plus extractor consistency (two uncached runs) |

The offline stub extractor maps keywords to canonical claims and the fake embedder hashes
words. They check logic, not thresholds. Absolute thresholds that depend on real embeddings
(for example the `N_sem ≥ 0.9` check, or a paraphrase scoring < 0.2) are live tests only.

## 8. Visualization

The Streamlit app ([app.py](../app.py), `streamlit run app.py`) implements the views
specified in [VISUALIZATIONS.md](VISUALIZATIONS.md), all computed from real pipeline output
(screenshots in the [README](../README.md#visualizations)):

| View | Where | Answers |
|------|-------|---------|
| Gate checklist + score-component bars | Score tab | Why did this submission get this score? |
| Point-level LLM view | Score tab | What did the judge decide about each point (new, matched with m, article), and what weight followed? |
| Relevance vs novelty scatter | Visualizations tab | Do the gates block novel but irrelevant posts? (Off-topic items sit high on novelty, left of the relevance floor) |
| Novelty distribution | Visualizations tab | Where does a new comment fall against the pool (leave-one-out `N_sem`)? |
| Leaderboard | Visualizations tab | Which comments are most novel, and who was first with an idea? |

The current submitter's comment is always marked "You", even when a gate blocked it and it
never joined the pool. Pool rows come from `data/corpus_scores.json` (written by
`build-pool`). One simplification: pool relevance in the scatter is the cosine of the combined
headline + body embedding to the article (a proxy), while this session's submissions use the
exact 0.3·headline + 0.7·body relevance.

The UI also explains itself: each example button says, on hover and above the form once
loaded, what it demonstrates and its expected score; each stance option shows its meaning.

## 9. Limitations

Measured evidence is in [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md) §3–4.

1. **Mean over points over-credits a single "new" sub-point.** This was the cause of all
   three holdout failures. A mostly repeated submission with one unmatched sub-point gets
   1/n credit.
2. **Persona-specific consequences count as new** ("faster deliveries", "more cab fares").
   Whether these are novel is debatable, but they let paraphrases escape the < 0.2 bar.
3. **Extraction is not repeatable** across uncached runs, even at temperature 0. Stored
   points keep scores stable, but a rebuild can shift them.
4. **Scores depend on arrival order** (first-mover credit). This is intended, but the same
   text can score differently on different days.
5. **λ = 0.4 is close to first-mover-only.** The 2nd and 3rd person to make a point are
   barely distinguished.
6. **Small evaluation sets** (23 dev, 19 holdout): one item changes a criterion.
7. **Cost and latency:** two LLM calls per submission that passes the gates. On free tiers
   this is limited by tokens per minute (about 1–2 submissions/minute on Groq).
8. **One article.** The calibration (floors, `t`, `dup_max`) is per article and must be
   redone for new content.
